Acronyms and definitions

Read this first
K/DKills divided by deaths. 1.00 means an even trade. Support operators sit lower by design — it is not a skill score.
WRWin rate: the share of rounds won on that operator, or matches won across a career. This is a team stat — it reflects who a player plays with as much as how they play. If a write-up says someone wins their rounds, it means this number, not K/D.
SRVSurvival rate: the share of rounds finished alive. A dead player contributes nothing for the rest of the round, so this moves win rate more than any other figure here.
1vXClutch wins: rounds won while outnumbered. 1v2, 1v3, 1v4 or 1v5. Counted per operator on stats.cc.
HS%Headshot percentage. The cleanest read on pure gun skill, because explosives and gadget kills cannot inflate it.
Entry +/−Opening kills minus opening deaths — the first kill and first death of each round. Gadget and explosive kills count, so always read it next to HS%.
RdsRounds played on that operator. Ten rounds or fewer is noise; 11–30 is a hint; 30+ starts to mean something.
RECRecommended, not proven. Ten rounds or fewer played, so the pick is based on playstyle and role fit rather than a settled result.
Outside playsMaps where attackers can meaningfully work from outside through windows or open sides. For Glaz, the useful pattern is strong outside-to-inside sightlines: Plane and Outback are proven, with Club House, Oregon, Chalet and Coastline worth testing.
SubstitutesOperators outside the top five that can still be picked. If that player has already put real rounds on them, they sit here as a bench. If not, substitutes (on trial) means there is not enough play yet to call them settled. Use Field Training where that operator has AI support; otherwise test in live matches.
Career vs SeasonEvery figure here is career unless labelled otherwise. Single seasons swing wildly; careers don't.

On sample size: a rate held over 1,000 matches is a harder and more reliable number than the same rate over 200. Small samples drift upward on a good run and collapse on a bad one; large ones settle on the truth. Read every figure below with its match count attached.

Tips

Small habits to squad protocols

Micro — do these without thinking

No decisions required. Just repetition until they're automatic.

  1. Crosshair at head height, permanently. It should be on the doorway before the doorway is on screen.
  2. Lean-peek, don't body-peek. Shoulder out, not whole silhouette.
  3. Reload after every kill, before moving — never when the mag runs dry, never in the open.
  4. Stand a step to the side of a doorway, not in it.
  5. Walk within one room of site. Sprint only when rotating.
  6. Park the drone alive in a safe corner after entry. Free late-round flank intel for nothing.
  7. Check floors and archways before entering. The original rule, still the highest-value one. Assume Kapkan on every frame you have not watched get opened. Shin height. When the drone has gone through the door, turn it around and look at the frame it just passed — especially if Kapkan is confirmed. Walking in after a drone that only looked forward is how the trap still hits.
  8. Clear the immediate threat before chasing the next one. The Deimos lesson. A callout twenty metres away does not matter while somebody is standing in the room you are in.
  9. Aim low with the Scorpion. On Denari the upward recoil is heavy enough that lower-body aim climbs into headshots on its own. Start at the chest or below and let the gun do the rest.
  10. Three losses in a row, stop for the night. No decision, no debate. Every long negative session in the log kept going well past the point it was salvageable.

Round — procedures inside a single round

These need a decision, but only one, and always the same one.

  1. One angle at a time. If you can be shot from two places at once, back out and re-approach.
  2. Move after every gunfight. Win or lose. Most casual deaths land 5–10 seconds after a kill, in the same spot.
  3. Stay in trade distance. If nobody can see the angle you're about to take, don't take it.
  4. Open rotations in prep phase. Every second spent making a hole mid-round is a second not shooting.
  5. Decide pre-plant or post-plant before entering. Mixing them is how kills go up and wins go down.
  6. One fight you start per round. After that, only fights that come to you or that the objective demands.
  7. Shoot gadgets, not just people. Batteries, ADS units, traps. Costs nothing, wins rounds.
  8. Roam with intel, anchor without it. The rule that fixes the most deaths across the whole squad.
  9. Enter where you know. Take the entry point you are comfortable with and work across to site. A familiar route entered early beats the perfect route entered late.
  10. Do not repeat an entry. The same rappel window twice in a match, or breaking windows in the same order every round, tells the defence where you will appear next. Vary it even when the first one worked.
  11. Flanks scale with room size. Roaming and flanking pays on small-room maps like Villa, Club House and House. On big interiors like Bank the flank crosses open ground, so hold an angle instead of running into one.
  12. Clear the room, not the route. The roamer behind you, the corner you walked past, and the hiding spot you skipped kill more rounds than the objective does. Every angle, including in training — treat them as people.
  13. Trap the doors nobody breaches. On maps where attackers walk through doorways because hard breaching is rare — Presidential Plane above all — Kapkan collects kills for no exposure at all.
  14. The objective sound is bait until proven otherwise. A plant or defuse noise is the most reliable way in the game to make somebody run into a prepared angle. It tells you roughly where a player is, not that they are occupied. Get to the last piece of cover, stop there, then peek or throw. Arriving late and alive beats arriving on time and dead.
  15. Numbers set the tempo on defence, and the defuser overrides both. Down a player, take the fight: a passive lone anchor gets isolated and traded one at a time, and only a pick resets the maths. Up a player, do nothing — the attackers have to come through you and the clock already belongs to you. Once the defuser is down that flips entirely and the defence has to move, whatever the count says.

Squad — protocols for the stack

None of these are individual skill, and they're the ones that move the win rate.

  1. Assign roles before the match, not per round. Five minutes of "yasbas10 is always breach, Big_Bunda_20 is always Lion" beats any individual improvement here.
  2. Protect the planter. Kulan_G100's job is reaching the site alive with the defuser. Somebody else takes first contact; two players watch the plant. He calls the spawn and who walks in with him, and he calls when a plant or swing is actually safe. Spending him on an unsupported entry is how the late round loses its planter. The gun is already there once the defuser is down.
  3. Call it out — all five of you. Comms are a squad habit, not one player's job. Five people trading accurate callouts beats five people shooting well in silence.
  4. Plan the entry out loud in prep phase. Name the wall, the breacher, who goes in behind him, which peep-holes get made and who watches them. The rounds this squad wins are the ones where that conversation happened; the losses are the improvised ones.
  5. Enter behind the hard breach, not beside it. yasbas10 opens the wall, somebody else takes first contact, then he trades. His opening record on Ace is poor, and the squad needs him alive to finish the breach.
  6. Never two people on one angle. One is wasted and both die to the same grenade.
  7. Fuze never deploys after the defuser is down. Cluster charges destroy a planted defuser and kill teammates in the room below. Call the location out loud before deploying, every time.
  8. Rotate off an angle once it has been peeked. Holding the same spot twice is how anchors get found.
  9. Pick tight sites. With Castle, Kaid, Azami and Valkyrie the squad is built for enclosed, hole-heavy sites.
  10. Cut the operator pool. Playing fewer operators, and repeating them, is how a line gets reliable. Spreading rounds across a dozen picks at once is how nothing settles.
  11. Nobody owns anti-throwables, and that is the biggest gap in the squad. Wamai has never been played on any of the five accounts, and the combined Jäger record across all of them is a handful of rounds at a very poor K/D. Every drone, grenade and breach charge that reaches site currently arrives unopposed. eshwador already carries Jäger or Wamai as a substitute on trial and is the natural owner. Until somebody owns it, whoever sees the grenades coming takes Jäger for the round. Kulan_G100 did exactly that on the 26th, and it paid.
  12. Gadget clear is covered three ways. Kulan_G100 now takes Twitch when he is the enabler: shock the denial, then get off the drone. Flores still has the most rounds on Big_Bunda_20. On Niro__99 the pick is Flores, because he already keeps drones alive and hidden. The F2 is not a fit on his account.
  13. Spend their bans on the oppressive picks. Denari and Fenrir on defence, Dokkaebi on attack. Play them loud enough that two of the three bans land there, and Kapkan, Bandit or Kaid, Mute, Thorn and Skopós or Echo walk free. Win early as well, because a ban that arrives in the next match only matters if there is a next match.
  14. Write the plan before the drone phase. A named attack, with jobs, is worth more than a better mechanical night. Smoky bacon is the proof. The maps that still feel like a blank page are the ones that need a name next.
